## Findings

1. **major** `page.chart_view.title`, `page.chart_view.key[0].note`: `HT` is presented as NESO naming it Holistic Transition, but NESO's FES 2024 building-block file never carries the code `HT`. It carries full pathway names only (silver `fes_building_blocks_main`, edition 2024, `published_at` 2024-08-02: Counterfactual, Electric Engagement, Holistic Transition, Hydrogen Evolution). The only place NESO writes "Holistic Transition (HT), Electric Engagement (EE), Hydrogen Evolution (HE)" is the tRESP dictionaries (field `Pathway`, file last modified 2026-01-30), and the vault's own tRESP note calls those "tRESP adaptations, not FES editions". So `HT` = Holistic Transition rests on initials matching: plausible (EE, HE and HT are three of the four 2024 names) but our reading, and NESO's dictionary for this very file decodes only the 2021 codes. A numeric cross-check I tried (electrolyser block `Dem_BB009` against types `Z` and `T`) did not settle it, so claim no more than the initials. The key note is also undated on the page. Fix: word it as a reading, for example "Pathway `HT`, read as Holistic Transition: the initials of a pathway in NESO's FES 2024 building-block file (2 August 2024); this file's own dictionary does not decode it". The title can keep the name if the key note carries that caveat. The note body paragraph (the tRESP sentence) should say "read as", not "abbreviate".

2. **major** (writer's report, "Defects" section, not the page): wrong negative-value counts that the seat would paste into the gridflow backlog. The report says residential negatives are "678 to 1,877 rows per edition" and "in 2021 also commercial (248) and industrial (6,417)", and the chart section says "smallest -0.603 MW at label 50". Measured on silver (rows with any of the three measures below zero): 2021 8,542 (types R, C, I), 2022 1,312 (R), 2023 1,011 (R), 2024 678 (R). By measure: `DemandPk` negative on R only, 868 / 210 / 87 / 146 rows (2021 to 2024), minimum -0.625 MW (2024); `DemandAM` negative in 2021 on C 248, I 3,934, R 916; `DemandPM` negative in 2021 on I 6,231 (minimum -757.9 MW) and R 1,021, and on R in 2022 to 2024 at 1,312 / 924 / 678. Correct the defect text, and say which measure, before it is pasted.

3. **nit** `page.chart_view.title`, `key[0].label`: "Peak demand" reads as a national figure. NESO defines `DemandPk` as "expected demand at the specified location at the time of the highest total demand for the nation" and says nothing about whether it adds up, so the sum is gridflow's. The caption does say "summed over every location and demand type", which avoids a false claim, but the title and key label drop the word. Suggest "Summed peak demand" and a short "gridflow's sum" in the caption. (The coincident definition supports adding; 2024 has no blanks; the sum includes one slightly negative residential row per label, -0.085 to -0.133 MW, immaterial.)

4. **nit** `page.record.fields.demand_pk`: negative residential values are not mentioned anywhere on the page, although the chart sum includes one per bar (at most 0.133 MW) and NESO's dictionary gives no sign convention. One clause in the guide or caption ("a few small residential values are negative, as published") would do.

5. **nit** `page.record.fields.demand_pk`: "some direct connects blank before 2024" is true (114 `T` locations in each of the 2021 to 2023 files) but undated and unscoped. Say "blank on 114 direct connects in NESO's 2021 to 2023 files".

6. **nit** `page.record.fields.type`: decodes four of the eight codes the frame shows (H, D, T, Z); C, E, I and R (commercial, electric vehicles, industrial, residential) appear in the frame but not in the guide. Add them if the budget allows; NESO's dictionary gives all eight.

7. **nit** note body, "Six things to know" item 2 and "The year label": "the page never converts it to one [a calendar year]" and "gridflow does not add the century" sit beside a chart that shows "2025/26". The substance is consistent (NESO's own financial-year reading, not a calendar year), but add "the chart shows NESO's own financial-year reading" so the body does not seem to contradict the page.

8. **nit** `page.facts.history`: "NESO created the package on 6 July 2021" is right on the date (`metadata_created` 2021-07-06), but the package author field then reads National Grid Electricity System Operator. "The package was created on 6 July 2021" avoids the attribution.
