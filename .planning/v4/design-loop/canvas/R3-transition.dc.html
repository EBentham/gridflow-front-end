A pipeline and catalogue for UK & European energy data.

Gridflow ingests, normalises and serves time-series data from seven public and authenticated vendors (electricity, gas, weather and carbon) into a single queryable warehouse. Models for demand, wind, solar and clearing prices sit one layer above.

Seven vendors, 165 datasets, four markets, seventeen years of history, three pipeline layers.

The horizon is data. Its height is the gas share of GB generation, in per cent, from the NESO Data Portal dataset historic_generation_mix (column gas_pct), 1 Jan 2009 to 20 Aug 2026. Each of the 120 points averages about 54 days of half-hourly values; the pale line is a rolling mean over seven points, about 12 months. The turbines, solar rows, pylons, batteries and data centre are drawing, not data.

Probabilistic estimators for demand, wind, solar and clearing prices live in gridflow-models, a sibling repository: one LightGBM model per quantile, walk-forward validated, artifact-pinned to the data fingerprint that trained them. One model has shipped; four are planned.

Bronze, silver and gold

Everything is Hive-partitioned Parquet, queryable from DuckDB or the Python client. Backfills are idempotent. Schema changes are caught at parse time by Pydantic v2 contracts. Bitemporal: every row carries both event-time and ingestion-time, so point-in-time queries are trivial.

SQL or Python: pick one

All examples use the same canonical silver schema.

-- GB residual demand last 30 days, hourly
SELECT
  date_trunc('hour', settlement_dt) AS hour,
  sum(case when fuel_type = 'WIND'  then mw end) AS wind_mw,
  sum(case when fuel_type = 'NPSHYD' then mw end) AS hydro_mw,
  sum(mw) AS total_mw
FROM silver.fuelhh
WHERE settlement_dt >= now() - interval '30 days'
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;
