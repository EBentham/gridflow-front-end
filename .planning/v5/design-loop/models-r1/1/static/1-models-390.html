From demand to a day-ahead price

gridflow-models is a separate Python library that reads gridflow's silver tables. It forecasts GB demand, wind and solar, builds the GB supply stack, and clears demand against that stack for a day-ahead price.

Demand less wind and solar is residual demand; the price is where residual demand meets the supply stack.

It produces forecasts and prices for research. It does not produce orders, positions or P&L.

day_ahead.lgbm_demand.v1 v2

Day-ahead demand

Forecasts GB national demand for every half-hour of the next day, as a spread of quantiles in MW.

Version 1: median pinball 711.04 MW in walk-forward backtests.

How it works

LightGBM quantile regression, one model per quantile from 0.05 to 0.95, with a conformal widening of the outer band. Version 1 uses calendar terms and demand already published at issue time; version 2 adds weather.

Median pinball, 1 September 2024 to 22 August 2026: version 1 711.04 MW, version 2 599.72 MW. Version 2 is scored with observed weather standing in for the forecast, so its score is optimistic.

Pinball loss at the median is half the mean absolute error of the median forecast, in MW.

In a notebook: models.demand_forecast, models.demand_forecast_v2

wind.lgbm_quantile.v1

Wind generation

Forecasts GB metered wind output for every half-hour of the next day, from weather at twelve wind farm sites.

Seven quantiles of wind output per half-hour, in MW.

How it works

LightGBM quantile regression on hub-height wind speed, direction and air density at twelve sites, scored against a fitted power-curve baseline. Elexon's WINDFOR forecast is kept as a reference.

In a notebook: models.wind_forecast

solar.lgbm_quantile.v1

Solar generation

Forecasts GB embedded solar output for every half-hour of the next day, from weather at six solar sites.

Seven quantiles of solar output per half-hour, in MW.

How it works

LightGBM quantile regression on irradiance, cloud and sun position at six sites, fitted and scored on daylight half-hours against an irradiance-curve baseline. The target is NESO's estimate of embedded solar, not every GB solar generator.

In a notebook: models.solar_forecast

stack.gb.v1

GB merit-order stack

Builds the GB supply curve for one half-hour: available plant ranked by short-run marginal cost, in cumulative MW.

Constructive: it is rebuilt for each half-hour and has no parameters to fit.

How it works

Built on request from the unit register, outage notices and fuel prices, with nothing to fit. Fuel and carbon prices are synthetic. Clearing prices are floored at −500 GBP/MWh.

A supply curve: each unit's available MW and marginal cost, sorted, with cumulative MW.

In a notebook: models.stack.build(as_of)

fundamentals_smp.gb.v1

Fundamentals SMP

Clears residual demand against the stack every half-hour for a GB day-ahead price, scored against Elexon's market index.

Its published run clears recorded demand, wind and solar, so each half-hour gets one price, not a spread.

How it works

Residual demand is demand less wind and solar, less signed hydro, interconnector, OTHER and pumped storage flows. The price is the marginal cost where that demand meets the stack, floored at −500 GBP/MWh. The price model also accepts the three forecasts in place of the recorded values.

Published run, 18 August to 3 September 2026: mean bias −151.80 GBP/MWh and MAE 151.80 GBP/MWh against elexon/mid (APXMIDP). Every half-hour clears below the market index.

A perfect-prognosis backtest: recorded demand, wind and solar stand in for forecasts. Fuel and carbon prices are synthetic. APXMIDP mixes day-ahead and intraday trades. Clearing is floored at −500 GBP/MWh, which pulls some daily means below zero.

In a notebook: models.fundamentals_smp.backtest(...)
