## 4. New nits (none blocking)

### A. [nit] `page.what_it_is`: the fetch-timing sentence has no stated scope

"`forecast_demand` the forecast host, and silver keeps no fetch time for it. Of those rows, about one in seven were fetched before
the hour, up to five days ahead; the rest up to 373 hours after."

- "Of those rows" has no clear antecedent, and the figures are a measurement of the rows behind this site, not a rule: a reader's own
  silver, built from their own fetches, will differ. Rubric 3 forbids local row statistics; I take it the seat has ruled this
  disclosure in, so I do not raise it above a nit. The seat's own carried-over rule is to scope observations ("as sent in the
  responses we hold"), and this sentence is the one place that is not scoped.
- The page also no longer says why most rows were fetched late (the connector sends the window unchanged, so a window that starts
  before the fetch returns past hours); one clause would make the number read as a consequence, not an oddity.
- Suggested wording within the same space: "`forecast_demand` reads the forecast host, and silver keeps no fetch time for it. In the
  rows behind this site, about one in seven were fetched before their hour, up to five days ahead; the rest up to 374 hours after,
  because the connector sends the window as given."

### B. [nit] `page.how_used[2]`: "The `forecast_demand` request as a template for capturing real forecasts."

Not false, but it is advice rather than a use of the data, and "capturing" a real forecast is only possible in bronze (fetch times
live only there). Optional: "`forecast_demand` fetched on the day, with the fetch time kept in bronze only." or drop the bullet.

### C. [nit] "up to 373 hours after"

The measured maximum is 373.5 hours; "373" rounds it down by half an hour. "About 374" (or "about 15 days") is accurate.
